In D&D 5e, the Way of the Kensei monk gains Path of the Kensei at 3rd level, which lets you select two weapons as kensei weapons, at least one melee. Those become the chassis for everything the subclass does. Agile Parry works only when you make an unarmed strike after attacking with a melee kensei weapon, granting +2 AC until your next turn. Kensei's Shot, a bonus action, adds 1d4 to a ranged kensei weapon attack. Deft Strike spends 1 ki to add your Martial Arts die to one kensei weapon hit, once per turn.

The trap is choosing for flavor. A monk uses Martial Arts only with unarmed strikes and monk weapons, meaning simple melee weapons or shortswords, no two-handed or heavy property. Pick a longsword as your melee kensei weapon and you can swing it accurately, but you lose the bonus-action unarmed strike that Martial Arts grants, and with it the Agile Parry loop. Your repeatable defense evaporates for a bigger single die.

Take a weapon that is both a monk weapon and a kensei weapon. The shortsword qualifies, keeping Martial Arts, Agile Parry, and Deft Strike online together.

**At the table:** Name a shortsword and a longbow as your two kensei weapons at 3rd level.
