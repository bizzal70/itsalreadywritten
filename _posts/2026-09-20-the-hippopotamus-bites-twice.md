In Shadowdark, the Hippopotamus attacks twice per turn, each bite landing at +4 for 1d10 damage. That is up to 20 points from a single creature the party assumed was scenery near the riverbank. The math does not care how peaceful it looked.

The stat block also grants advantage on Strength checks to grab or drag. That clause is the real threat. A hippo that closes distance does not just chew, it hauls. Water is its terrain, and a grabbed character dragged into it trades solid footing for a bad afternoon. Clustered marching order makes this worse, since both bites can find targets without the creature moving much.

The common mistake is reading "animal" as "harmless" and letting players wade in loose formation. The correction is positional. Keep spacing, keep distance, and treat the water line as the edge of a trap rather than a backdrop.

**At the table:** When a PC ends their turn adjacent to the hippo and the water, have it bite, then use its advantaged Strength check to drag the grabbed character into deeper water, isolating them from the rest of the party.
