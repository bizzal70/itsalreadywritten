An Encounter Seed is not a stat block you drop and roll for. It is the reason the fight exists, the pressure it puts on the table, and the choice it forces before initiative even matters. Most groups treat a monster as an obstacle to be reduced to zero hit points. The better read is to ask what the creature is testing. Shadowdark's roster rewards this. The numbers are lean, so the interesting work happens in how you frame the threat and what the party gives up to survive it. Five creatures make the point cleanly.

Start with the Basilisk, because it earns its stakes fast. The danger is not its attack line, it is the DC 15 Constitution save against petrification triggered by gaze or touch. Players routinely underestimate how rarely they pass a save that high, and they get caught flat. The Basilisk also carries a Dexterity of 1, which is the whole tell. Keep your distance, refuse the eye contact, and open with ranged attacks that exploit that abysmal Dexterity. Seed it as a warning about preparation, not a brawl.

The Swarm Spider runs the same logic from the other direction. With AC 13 and only 9 hit points, it looks like a chump, and that is the trap. The real threat is the bite, which forces a DC 12 Constitution save or paralysis for 1d4 rounds. Fixating on its low hit points is the mistake. The correct play is stacking multiple attacks in a single round so the swarm dies before anyone drops. Open with the stakes, then let it pressure the table into a costly decision about who commits and who hangs back.

The Gargoyle is your values test. Give the table two wins and make them choose which one they can keep, because they will not have the resources for both. Its Armor Class of 16 and its flight make it hard for lower-level parties to touch, and two claws at +3 each for 1d6 grind down anyone who stays too long. The impervious talent shuts down non-magical attacks entirely, so the honest answer is often magic or a bypass. Seeded well, the Gargoyle asks whether the objective is worth the spell slots.

The Kraken is the playful one that still punishes bad habits. Its storm churns violently turbulent seas in a one-mile radius for 2d4 rounds, and the party that ignores capsizing gets exactly what it deserves when the ship goes over mid-combat. Prepare for the capsize or avoid the storm. The Kraken is not there to be beaten so much as respected, and it teaches that terrain is a combatant.

The Mastiff closes the set as comic relief that quietly steers decisions. A 1d6 bite at +1 to hit is not a real offensive threat, and its movement range of near is easy to misjudge in the heat of a turn. Groups waste actions when the Mastiff simply cannot reach its target. Use it as a distraction or tie it to an objective that does not demand sustained combat. The joke is that a harmless creature can still cost you a round if you sleep on positioning.

Read across all five and the lesson holds. The Basilisk and Swarm Spider punish misread numbers. The Gargoyle and Kraken punish overcommitment. The Mastiff punishes sloppy geometry. None of them is really about hit points. Each is a question about what the party knows, what it will spend, and where it stands.

**Running it:** Before you place any of these, write one sentence naming the choice it forces, and run the encounter until that choice gets made rather than until something dies.
