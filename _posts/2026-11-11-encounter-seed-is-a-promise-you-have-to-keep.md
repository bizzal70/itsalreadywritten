An Encounter Seed is not a monster slot. It is a promise about what the table will have to solve. The problem is that most judges plant the seed and then run the generic version of whatever grew from it, ignoring the exact mechanics printed on the stat block. The result is an encounter that reads threatening and plays limp, because the parts that make it dangerous never came up. Five creatures show how this failure happens, and how to avoid it.

Start with Athasi. The temptation is to run it as a sack of hit points, because 8d8+20 and an AC of 15 invite exactly that reading. Players see the cloak of concealment as flavor, not defense, and they forget that infravision out to 120' means Athasi has already seen them and chosen its ground. The real teeth are in its spellcasting. Athasi's charm person ignores the usual check penalties that apply when a non-humanoid monster casts it, which means the spell lands harder than anyone expects. Run Athasi for stakes and the lesson is plain: disrupt the casting first, treat the body second.

The Wood Door Golem makes the opposite error tempting. It has 0' movement, so players discount it as scenery. Then the slam connects for 2d6+3 and starts a grapple, and the stationary thing suddenly owns whoever walked too close. Worse, parties waste rounds hacking at it with steel, because it takes half damage from non-magical weapons. The printed vulnerability is fire and heat, which deals double, and its magic resistance imposes a 50% chance of spell failure, so leaning on a caster to burn it is unreliable. Charge the Wood Door Golem a real price, and the price is paid in non-spell fire sources: torches, oil, anything that does not roll against that resistance.

The Gargantuan Lizard earns its stakes fast because it closes fast. With 40' movement and a 20' climb speed, it is on the party before a plan forms, and its bite does 3d4 and carries a swallow whole mechanic that nobody respects until a character is already inside it. Durability compounds this with 10d8 hit dice behind AC 17. The exploitable seam is its Reflex save of +8, lower than it looks, which rewards ranged attacks and save-targeting spells before it arrives in melee.

Girtablullu threatens the formation rather than the individual. Its fearful gaze forces a Will save at DC 15, and a failed save sends characters backward, breaking the line and exposing the allies who held. The damage is almost beside the point; the collapse is the attack. The counter is positional and preparatory: buff Will saves or secure rerolls so that one bad roll does not unravel the whole fight.

The Boneshard Swarm of Bones should run on a hard clock, where indecision costs more than any single hit. It moves only 20', so it struggles to chase, and players read that as safety. The oversight is its blindsight. Stealth does not work against it, and parties who try to sneak past make positioning errors that pin them in place while the swarm grinds forward. The discipline is to keep distance and stop trusting cover you cannot actually hide behind.

Across all five, the lesson is the same. The Encounter Seed is only a threat if the judge runs the specific mechanic that makes it one: Athasi's charm, the Wood Door Golem's fire vulnerability, the Gargantuan Lizard's swallow, Girtablullu's gaze, the swarm's blindsight. Skip those and you have planted the seed and refused to let it grow.

**Running it:** Before the fight, circle the one printed mechanic the players will most likely overlook, and commit to using it the first round it applies.
