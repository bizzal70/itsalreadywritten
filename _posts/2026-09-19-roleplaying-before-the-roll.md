The Player's Handbook (chapter 8, "Using Ability Scores," and chapter 7's social interaction guidance) defines roleplaying as deciding how a character thinks, acts, and speaks. That is the descriptive layer. It does not, by itself, tell you what happens. The mechanical layer sits underneath: a Charisma (Persuasion), Charisma (Deception), or Charisma (Intimidation) check against a DC the DM sets, typically 10 for a favorable listener, 15 for indifferent, 20 for hostile per the DMG's social interaction table.

The disputes are almost never about the number rolled. They come from an unresolved sequence. A player narrates a threat, then wants a Persuasion check on the same breath, then adds a bribe after seeing the die. Two mechanics collide because their order was never fixed. The rules assume the DM establishes what the character is attempting first, then calls for one check that fits that stated action, then applies the result. Roleplaying frames the attempt; it does not stack multiple checks retroactively.

Lock the declared action before dice touch felt. Approach is stated, one skill is chosen, one d20 is rolled against one DC.

**At the table:** When a player says "I intimidate him, roll Persuasion?", stop, ask which action they are committing to, then call for that single check.
