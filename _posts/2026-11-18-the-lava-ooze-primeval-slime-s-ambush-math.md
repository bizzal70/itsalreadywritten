In DCC, the Lava Ooze Primeval Slime moves 5 feet per round. That number convinces most judges the thing is harmless, a slow hazard the party strolls past. The number that matters is the other one: +10 to stealth checks in volcanic terrain. In the right environment it is invisible until it touches you, and a 5-foot mover that ambushes is not slow, it is patient.

The pseudopod hits for 1d4 plus burning touch. Single strike, unremarkable. The danger is the stacking burn when the party ignores the creature and keeps marching, taking repeated touches from something they never spotted. Compounding that, it sits at -3 initiative, so once combat starts the players almost always act first. That is not the Slime's weakness working against it. That is the window it hands you to end things before it accumulates damage.

It takes double from cold and water. With a 5-foot move it cannot chase a positioning plan, so controlling where it sits is trivial once it is seen. The whole fight turns on detection, not damage.

**At the table:** Call for passive Luck or a Will check against the camouflage before the party enters the volcanic tile, not after the first burning touch lands.
