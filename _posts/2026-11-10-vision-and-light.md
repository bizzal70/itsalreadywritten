In D&D 5e, the Vision and Light rules define three states: bright light, dim light, and darkness. Dim light is a lightly obscured area, which imposes disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight. Darkness is a heavily obscured area, and inside it a creature effectively suffers from the blinded condition against anything it cannot see.

The blinded condition is where the real penalty lives. A blinded creature has disadvantage on attack rolls, and attack rolls against it have advantage. So a fighter firing a longbow across a pitch-black room does not get a free shot at disadvantage because of "darkness" in the abstract. They get disadvantage because they are, mechanically, blind to the target. Darkvision cancels this by letting you treat darkness as dim light within its radius, though you still see that area in shades of gray, which means it remains lightly obscured for Perception.

Light sources settle it fastest. A torch sheds bright light in a 20 foot radius and dim light for another 20 feet, turning a heavily obscured kill box into a merely inconvenient one.

**At the table:** A player with no Darkvision shoots into true darkness. Rule it as blinded: disadvantage on the attack, and anything shooting back at them has advantage.
