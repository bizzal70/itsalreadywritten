The Athasi's cloak of concealment invites the wrong read. Judges file it under ambush flavor and move on. Meanwhile it carries infravision out to 120 feet, which means it has usually spotted the party, picked its angle, and begun working before anyone rolls initiative. The concealment is not the threat. The sightline is.

The stat block reinforces the misjudgment. With 8d8+20 hit points and AC 15, it looks like something you trade blows with until one side falls over. That framing ignores its spell list. The dangerous line is charm person, which in DCC skips the usual check penalties that apply when the target is a non-humanoid monster. Against the Athasi, that caveat does not shield your party. It converts one of your own into a liability mid-fight.

So the Athasi is an encounter built on tempo and conversion, not attrition. Run it for the stakes it actually produces: a creature that sees first, opens with control, and lets the players' own front line do the damage. Flavor it however you like, but the pressure comes from the spell, not the fiction.

**At the table:** The moment the Athasi starts casting charm person, make disrupting it the party's declared priority, by damage, grapple, or interruption, before the saving throw lands.
