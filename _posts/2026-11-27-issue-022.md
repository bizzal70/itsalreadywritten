*Issue #022 · Week of November 27, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The big news is a company doing something decent with money it did not lose: Wizards of the Coast will pass its share of a $1.3 billion Anthropic settlement to the authors whose work got scraped. Everything else is Black Friday noise, a D&D Beyond hack, and the ongoing WoW class port that EN World is covering class by class as if we might forget. A quiet week dressed up in sale banners.

## Releases & Announcements

**D&D Beyond** launched a fiction line, starting with digital editions of **R.A. Salvatore's "Homeland"** and **"Dragons of Autumn Twilight"** bundled with bonus playable content for 5.5E, per **Dungeons & Dragons Fan**. The timing is awkward given the same platform is reportedly dealing with a hack, noted in **EN World's Podcast #424** rundown. A **Shadowheart** novel by **Tasha Suri** is coming in 2027.

**WizKids** revealed **"Elminster's Areas of Effect,"** pre-painted official spell templates for battlemaps, which is one way to settle the eternal argument about where the fireball actually lands. On the personnel side, **Amanda Hamon** was promoted to Principal Game Designer of D&D's Universes Beyond line, which includes the **World of Warcraft** crossover. EN World continues its class-by-class WoW previews: Evoker, Mage, Monk, Paladin, and Priest, with Evokers landing as Sorcerers and the recommended Mage build being, inexplicably, an Evoker Wizard.

Elsewhere, **Exalted Funeral** has **Mystic Punks** on the way, an OSR game about magical crustpunks, reviewed at EN World by **Egg Embry**. **UO Publishing** put out **Harrow: The Blighted Plane**, a crystalpunk corebook on DriveThruRPG. Print news from EN World covers supplements for **Wrath & Glory**, **Delta Green**, **Fear Itself**, **Draw Steel**, and **Call of Cthulhu**.

## Rules & Errata

No official errata from **Wizards**, **The Arcane Library**, or **Goodman Games** this week. What passes for rules content is comparative: **Ruckerworks** published a "switching to Shadowdark from 5e" piece covering five-minute character creation and the real-time torch timer, and asked whether it is worth it. **Periapt Games** circulated an **Albom (2021)** study finding that 5e's text leans hard on violence, with "combat" appearing roughly six times as often as "exploration" and 62% of spells coded as violent. Read it as a design critique, not a patch note.

## The Community

The **Anthropic settlement** passthrough is the story people should actually be talking about, and credit to EN World for keeping it in frame across several items. Instead, much oxygen went to **Critical Role**: **Aabria Iyengar** and **Travis Willingham** are returning after months away, and someone on dice.camp is hunting for **The Black Rabbits** stat block that CR released to Beacon subscribers.

The OSR corner produced the better reading. **Domain of Many Things** answered an r/rpg player tired of fighting every encounter with the usual sound advice: make combat deadly but fair, telegraph danger, show the random encounter table, and reward players who walk away. **phd20** argued that 5e supporting every kind of story does not mean your campaign should try to, and the German-language scene spun up two **Mausritter** hacks including a genuinely committed "Teenage Mutant Turtle Ritters." **Cairn 2e** got a German translation of its player's guide. **Sly Flourish** offered the week's most durable table tip: take turns around the table to spread the spotlight, and recap the group's objective at session start.

One for the historians: **The Contrarian** reminded everyone that **Troll Lord Games** once crowdfunded a **Castles & Crusades** TV show that produced a four-minute short and nothing else. The lesson, as he put it, is that "TV show based on a game based on D&D" may simply not be a plan.

## On the Horizon

Crowdfunding had its loudest year on record. **Sword World** became 2026's 14th million-dollar TTRPG crowdfunder, per EN World, which is either a sign of health or a sign nobody is pricing risk anymore. **Prophecy of Doom**, a Tarot-driven OSR campaign toolset for **Shadowdark** and **MÖRK BORG**, is running via BackerKit, and the **Argosa Emergent Play Sandbox**, a sword-and-sorcery OSR box set pitched at 5e refugees, is live on Kickstarter.

**Chaosium** is premiering its Bayeux-themed project at a British Museum event, and **Paizo** announced Arcadia sourcebooks. EN World is also taking nominations for most anticipated RPG of 2027, so start arguing early. On the convention front, **Tabletop Scotland** set dates through 2029, returning September 10 to 12, 2027.

And yes, the Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales are still running at **DriveThruRPG**, **Azukail Games**, **Ruckerworks**, and plenty of itch.io storefronts, including a bundle literally titled "everything must go." Buy what you will actually run.
