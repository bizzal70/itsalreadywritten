The Pirate in Shadowdark carries 4 hit points and an AC of 12. On paper, it dies to a single good hit. Cutlass for 1d6, dagger for 1d4, both at close or near range. Read the numbers alone and you conclude the thing is trivial.

The numbers alone lie. A Pirate is not a solo stat block, it is a seed. One is a nuisance. The threat is never the Pirate you are fighting, it is the count of rounds you spend fighting it instead of reaching the hold, cutting the line, or taking the deck. Fragility invites you to grind through them one by one, which is exactly the trap: every round of delay is a round the rest of the crew uses to arrive.

So treat the encounter as a clock, not a health bar. The question is not whether you can kill a Pirate (you can) but how many turn up before the objective is met. Measure your progress in minutes, not corpses.

**At the table:** Set a timer for three real minutes when the fight starts. Every full minute that expires, one more Pirate steps into the scene at near range, and keep adding them until the party completes the actual goal.
