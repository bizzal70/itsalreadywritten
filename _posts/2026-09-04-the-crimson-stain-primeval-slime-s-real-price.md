In Dungeon Crawl Classics, the Crimson Stain Primeval Slime occupies multiple 5-foot squares, each with its own action die. That detail gets read as flavor and treated as a single fat hit-point pool. It is neither. It is several attackers wearing one name.

The smother condition is the part players discount. It is not a damage line. It restricts movement and actions, which means the creature is not trying to kill you in one round so much as pin you until the math turns bad. Layer that against the slime's split ability and the standard response (hit it harder) becomes the trap. Every solid blow risks dividing one problem into two, each still carrying its own action die and its own smother. Damage output, absent area control and mobility, feeds the encounter rather than ending it.

The correct answer is spatial. Control the squares, deny the spread, and keep bodies out of smothering range before committing to the kill. The slime rewards patience and punishes the party that stands still and swings.

**At the table:** When the players bunch up in a corridor to concentrate fire, split the slime into the adjacent squares and open smother attacks on the packed front rank, so standing still costs them their actions.
