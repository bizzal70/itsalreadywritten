In the Dungeon Crawl Classics tradition of making magic items feel less like equipment and more like unreliable business partners, Medusa's Touch Blade forces a Fortitude save on a hit. Fail, and the target turns to stone. The catch is the DC: it is not fixed. You roll 1d10+10 each time, so the save your enemy faces ranges from a merciful 11 to a brutal 20.

That variance is the whole character of the weapon. A low roll turns a legendary petrifying blade into an ordinary sword with extra steps. A high roll ends encounters. Treating every swing as equivalent ignores that the item is quietly handing you a spread of outcomes and expecting you to read it.

The practical consequence is restraint. Random enemies and chaff do not justify the gamble, since a middling DC is wasted on things you could have hit normally. The blade earns its keep against the one target whose removal matters, where a 20 on the Fort save is close to a sentence.

**At the table:** Hold the blade for the fight's anchor enemy, roll the 1d10+10 openly, and if it lands at 18 or higher, make that the swing that stops the encounter.
