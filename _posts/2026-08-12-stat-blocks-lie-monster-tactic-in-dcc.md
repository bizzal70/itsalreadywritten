The hit dice line is the first thing most judges read and the least useful thing they act on. A monster's real threat lives in its attack routine, its movement, and the saves it forces after it hits. Monster Tactic is the discipline of reading a stat block for what it does across three rounds rather than how much damage it can absorb. Four creatures make the point cleanly, because each one is either overrated or underrated the moment a table glances at its numbers.

Start with the Hellthorn, because it is the honest one. Its 10d12 hit dice and AC 20 announce a serious fight, and its 0' movement tells you it will not chase. That combination invites a lazy read: park it, whittle it down. The trap is the tendril attack, which does not just deal 1d6 damage. It grapples, and it drips poison sap that drains 1d4 Stamina. The Stamina drain is the actual weapon. Every point lost erodes Fort saves and staying power, so a grappled fighter gets weaker precisely while stuck in range. The tactic against a Hellthorn is the tactic the Hellthorn hands you: it cannot move, so refuse to close. Ranged attacks and distance turn a formidable statline into a stationary problem.

The Ice Lurker teaches the opposite lesson, which is that the fight begins before anyone knows there is one. Its camouflage grants a +10 bonus to hide when it mimics frozen water, so the first data point a party receives is a pseudopod attack landing at +5 for 2d6 damage from a square nobody was watching. Worse, it is immune to non-magical, non-bludgeoning weapons and to cold, so the reflexive swing accomplishes nothing. The stat block also prints the answer in plain sight: vulnerability to fire and heat means double damage. A judge should run the Ice Lurker on frozen terrain specifically so the fire spell becomes obvious and rewarding.

Kaundyuk is the underestimated one, and it survives on the free turns a table gives it. It looks harmless, its 6d12 hit dice look modest, and both readings are mistakes. The real block is a bite at +8 for 1d8 plus poison and two claws at +8 for 1d8+2 each, all landing on a critical range of 19-20 with a DN/d6 crit die. That is three high-accuracy attacks per round with an inflated crit chance. Fixating on its hit dice and planning to outlast it means eating that barrage repeatedly. The counter is action economy: stun it, trip it, or blow through it fast, because every round it acts unimpeded is the round it earns its reputation.

The Hellseeker Cockatrice rounds out the set as low-threat on paper and high-chaos at the table. It is sturdier than its level suggests, with 3d8 hit dice and AC 14. The peck deals only 1d3 damage, but it forces a DC 13 Will save against petrification, and losing a character to stone is not a hit-point problem. The tail adds another 1d3 with a blood loss effect gated behind a DC 12 Fort save, so it stacks ongoing damage on top of the petrification threat. Its 30' flying movement lets a judge dictate range and keep both clocks running.

The through line is simple. The Hellthorn's Stamina drain, the Ice Lurker's ambush and vulnerability, Kaundyuk's action economy, and the Hellseeker Cockatrice's paired saves are all details the hit dice actively obscure. Read the routine, not the pool.

**Running it:** For every monster, write down the one thing that happens after it hits, and let that line decide how you position it.
