The Roper looks like a stalagmite until it isn't. Its threat lives in the tendrils, not the mouth. A tendril grabs on a hit, and the grabbed creature is stuck until it wins a DC 15 Strength check or the tendril is cut. Miss that check and the Roper reels you in, at which point the 2d8 bite finally becomes relevant.

The trap most parties fall into is treating this as a single monster with a scary bite. It isn't. It's a monster with several ropes, each one a separate problem, and each grabbed ally is a turn spent failing a Strength check instead of fighting. Free-action heroics don't help. You cut the tendril or you don't move.

So the price is attention. Players who focus fire on tendrils survive. Players who ignore the tendrils to swing at the body get walked into the mouth one by one, like a bad afternoon on a pier.

**At the table:** When a tendril hits, call for the DC 15 Strength check immediately and narrate the slack going tight; make the players decide, out loud, whether they spend actions cutting rope or freeing the guy already halfway to the teeth.
