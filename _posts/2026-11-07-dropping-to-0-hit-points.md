The rule in the Player's Handbook is precise. When damage reduces a creature to 0 hit points, it either falls unconscious or, if enough damage remains, dies outright. Instant death triggers only when the leftover damage (after the amount needed to drop you to 0) equals or exceeds your hit point maximum. A commoner with 4 HP and a 6-maximum who takes 11 damage is simply dead. A barbarian with the same 4 HP remaining but a 60-maximum who takes 11 takes the 4 to reach 0, has 7 left over, and 7 does not beat 60. That barbarian is unconscious and rolling death saves.

The mechanic is symmetrical. Monsters calculate instant death the same way players do, using the monster's own hit point maximum from its stat block. Nothing in the rules grants enemies a different math or a softer landing.

That symmetry is the whole point. If you let a villain shrug off a blow that would have flat-killed a PC, you have changed the rule mid-combat, and the table will notice.

**At the table:** Before a boss fight, write the monster's HP maximum next to its current HP, so when a critical lands you can check the overkill threshold without inventing a ruling under pressure.
