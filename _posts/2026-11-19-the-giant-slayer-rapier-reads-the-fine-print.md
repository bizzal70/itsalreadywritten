In D&D 5e, the Giant Slayer (Rapier) is a +1 weapon: +1 to attack rolls, +1 to damage rolls. That part applies to anything you stab. The headline, however, is conditional.

The extra 2d6 damage and the knockdown trigger only against creatures of the Giant type. On a hit against a Giant, the target takes the additional 2d6 and must make a DC 15 Strength saving throw or fall Prone. Against an ogre, a troll, a hill giant, this is excellent. Against a dragon, a demon, a bandit captain, it is a +1 rapier and nothing more. Giant type is a specific line on the stat block, not a size category, so a Huge creature that is not a Giant gets nothing special done to it.

The common misplay is budgeting for 2d6 that never arrives, then wondering why the big target did not go down. The weapon is named honestly. It slays Giants. It merely pokes everyone else.

**At the table:** When a player swings it at something, check the creature's type line first. If it does not read "Giant," no bonus dice and no Strength save, just the flat +1.
