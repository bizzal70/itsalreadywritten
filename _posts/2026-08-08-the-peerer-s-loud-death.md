The Peerer is built around a conflict of priorities. It attacks with eye rays, which is what draws attention, but its real function is as a fey sentinel: when intruders come near, it raises an alarm. Kill it slow and you win the fight while losing the dungeon, because everything downstairs now knows you are coming.

The math argues for haste. Armor class 10 means most attacks land. Movement of 20 feet means it cannot outrun anything, and it presents an easy target for ranged fire. The exploitable detail is its vulnerability to fire, which deals double damage. A creature that fragile does not survive a committed opening volley, and a dead sentinel triggers no alarm.

So the encounter is a race, not a slugfest. The threat is not the Peerer's damage output. The threat is the round in which it stays alive long enough to sound off. Every turn spent trading blows conventionally is a turn it spends calling for help.

**At the table:** Open on the Peerer with a fire attack from range before melee closes, banking on double damage to drop it in one exchange and silence the alarm.
