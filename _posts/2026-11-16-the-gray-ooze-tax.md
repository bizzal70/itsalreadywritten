In D&D 5e the Gray Ooze carries Armor Class 9 and 22 hit points, which reads as a corpse waiting to happen. It isn't. The Monster Manual gives it resistance to acid, cold, and fire damage, so the elemental artillery most parties reach for first lands at half value. The thing survives on the assumption that nobody checked its stat block.

The cleaner answer is damage it doesn't resist. Bludgeoning, piercing, slashing, force, radiant, lightning, thunder, psychic, poison, necrotic: all go through at full. A mace does more here than a Fireball, which is the kind of sentence that gets casters to read a resistance line for once. AC 9 means the fighter who stopped relying on cantrips rarely misses.

For the DM, the Ooze earns its keep by context. Drop it where elemental magic is the party's reflex (a scorched vault, a frozen cellar, anywhere they've been winning with fire) and let the resistances do the teaching. It is a low-CR creature that punishes autopilot, not optimization.

**At the table:** Place the Gray Ooze guarding a narrow flooded passage so the wizard's instinct to Fireball the choke point costs a round, then let the melee line close and end it with steel.
