The D&D 5e Druid (CR 2, Monster Manual) looks fragile on paper. AC 13, 44 hit points, a quarterstaff that barely registers. Players read those numbers and treat it as a cleanup target, which is exactly the read it rewards. The defensive stats are not the threat. The spell list is.

The Druid's Spellcasting is built to punish geometry. It knows Thunderwave, a 15-foot cube that hits everything inside on a failed Constitution save and shoves them ten feet. It knows Entangle and Call Lightning if you prepare them. Every one of these scales with how tightly the party stands together. A melee line that collapses into a corner to focus the "weak caster" is handing the Druid its best round. Multiattack with the staff is the filler. The area spells are the point.

Spacing is the counter. Keep ten feet between frontliners and the area spells catch one target instead of four. Drift, and the cube finds all of you.

**At the table:** When three or more PCs stack within a 15-foot square, have the Druid open with Thunderwave: 2d8 thunder on a failed DC 13 Con save, half on success, plus the ten-foot shove to scatter their formation before the fighter reaches it.
